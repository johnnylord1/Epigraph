# Built-in Theme Refactor Specification

## Design read

Epigraph is a dense desktop reading application. This is a preserve-mode redesign of its theme system, with Nocturne as the reference for hierarchy, restraint, shape language, and interaction states. Each built-in theme must retain its own palette identity.

- Design variance: 4
- Motion intensity: 3
- Visual density: 6
- Foundation: existing Svelte 5 CSS-variable design system

## Problem statement

Nocturne is the only built-in theme with a coherent relationship between background, surfaces, controls, borders, text, and accent. The other built-ins currently expose the older fallback material system.

Observed failures:

- Dark uses elevated gray surfaces too aggressively while controls remain darker, which reverses hierarchy in several components.
- AMOLED uses `#5a5a5a` and `#737373` as normal borders on black. Those boundaries have approximately 3:1 contrast against the canvas, so every container is outlined and the theme reads like an old web interface.
- Non-Nocturne themes inherit the old HUD geometry: 5px controls, 5px popovers, 3px status elements, 7px toasts, 5px chapter rows, 30px controls, and 3px progress bars.
- Light themes use warm surface layers but component fallbacks do not consistently distinguish content surfaces from interactive controls.
- Status colors are mostly inherited from the dark default contract, even in the light, midnight, and warm palettes.
- The visual evidence matrix validates paint and contrast, but it does not reject incoherent surface ladders, excessive AMOLED borders, or inverted control hierarchy.

## Scope

Refactor all built-in themes except the Nocturne palette:

- Dark
- AMOLED
- High Contrast
- Light
- Light Contrast
- Midnight
- Warm

Nocturne remains the visual baseline and its authored palette values remain unchanged.

## Shared material rules

All built-ins use the same modern material grammar:

- Cards use elevation only when they represent a distinct object.
- Default borders are low contrast. Strong borders are reserved for focus, selection, errors, and explicit high-contrast themes.
- Buttons and inputs are distinguishable from their parent surface without becoming dark holes or bright gray patches.
- Popovers and modals sit one elevation step above their owner.
- One accent family is used per theme.
- Success, warning, danger, and information colors remain semantic and do not borrow the theme accent.
- Default control geometry follows Nocturne: 28px controls, 8px control radius, 12px popovers, pill status elements, 12px toasts, 9px chapter rows, and 2px progress.
- AMOLED keeps true black for the canvas and primary content surfaces. Depth comes from near-black overlays and restrained state fills, not persistent outlines.
- High Contrast and Light Contrast may use stronger boundaries because that is their explicit purpose.

## Palette direction

### Dark

Neutral graphite without pale gray islands. The surface ladder stays close to the canvas and violet is used only for focus, selection, and primary actions.

### AMOLED

True black canvas, surface, raised surface, and cards. Overlay layers use near-black violet. Normal borders are nearly invisible; focus and selected states remain clear.

### High Contrast

Near-black surfaces, off-white text, and intentionally stronger dividers. Contrast is explicit but still uses the shared rounded geometry rather than rectangular outlined boxes.

### Light

Cool-neutral off-white surfaces with a restrained violet accent. Controls are slightly raised relative to their parent and never render as dark wells.

### Light Contrast

The light family with darker text and clearer boundaries. It remains spatially modern and avoids outlining every container.

### Midnight

Blue-black surfaces with a muted periwinkle accent. Neutral and semantic colors are cooled so they do not appear imported from Dark.

### Warm

Deep umber surfaces with muted amber. Borders remain brown-black and subtle. Status colors are adjusted for the warm canvas while preserving their meaning.

## Architecture

1. `src/lib/theme/contract.ts` remains the source of truth for built-in primitives, semantic colors, HUD geometry, metadata, and motion.
2. `src/styles/builtin-themes.generated.css` remains generated by `pnpm theme:generate-builtins` and is never hand-edited.
3. `src/styles/tokens.css` owns shared component fallbacks. These fallbacks will adopt the restrained Nocturne material rules while continuing to derive from theme primitives.
4. `src/styles/nocturne-theme.css` keeps Nocturne-specific authored overrides. It must not gain copies of the other built-in palettes.
5. Custom theme schema V5, IDs, persistence, routes, labels, component markup, and behavior remain unchanged.

## Quality gates

- Every built-in contract contains the complete primitive, semantic, HUD, and motion inventory.
- Dark theme surface luminance is monotonic from canvas to overlay and does not jump to pale gray.
- Light theme surface luminance is monotonic in the opposite direction and controls remain visible against their parent.
- AMOLED keeps `bg-void`, `bg-base`, `bg-surface`, `bg-raised`, `color-background`, `color-surface`, and `color-card` at `#000000`.
- AMOLED normal border contrast against black stays below 2:1. Focus contrast remains at least 3:1.
- Body text contrast remains at least 4.5:1 on background, cards, controls, inputs, toasts, modals, and popovers.
- No component token introduced by this refactor contains a direct color. Components consume primitives, semantics, or derived color sources.
- Empty V5 custom themes remain computed-equivalent to every built-in base.
- Nocturne computed component values remain unchanged.
- Browser evidence covers background, card, control, input, menu, modal, toast, focus, and semantic status samples for all eight built-ins.

## Out of scope

- Changing theme IDs or user-facing navigation.
- Changing the custom theme document schema.
- Redesigning page layouts or component markup.
- Changing Nocturne's palette.
- Adding a new dependency or design-system package.
- Modifying reader content themes such as sepia, EPUB prose themes, or PDF rendering modes.
